The access to justice problem

People freeze, and give up rights they never knew they had.

When an authority figure confronts you, stress takes over. The rules that protect you are written in legal language, and most people can't afford to call a lawyer in the moment. So they say too much, agree to things they don't have to, or stay quiet when they should speak up.

  • The law is written for lawyersThe Charter promises protection from unreasonable search and a right to a lawyer, but it never tells you the words to use [1].
  • Encounters are suddenYou can't research your rights during a traffic stop or a meeting with the vice-principal. Information has to arrive in seconds.
  • Young people feel the pressure mostThe Supreme Court of Canada has recognized that young and racialized people may not feel free to walk away from police, even when they legally can [3].
  • Help exists, but it comes laterOrganizations like Legal Aid Ontario, Justice for Children and Youth and the John Howard Society of Ontario support people after a problem starts [13] [14] [16]. Few tools help during the moment itself.
Everyday legal problems

Nearly half of adult Canadians face at least one everyday legal problem over a three-year period.

Canadian Forum on Civil Justice national survey [17]

The gap Pocket Rights fills

Legal information websites are detailed and accurate, but they are built for reading at home. Pocket Rights is built for the five seconds when you need the right sentence.

How Pocket Rights solves it

A calm guide in your pocket.

The name says it: legal help that lives on the phone in your pocket, dedicated to explaining and protecting your rights.

Situation scripts

Exact, polite lines that protect your rights and lower the temperature. Written in plain English and checked against the law.

Plain-language flashcards

The Charter, the Youth Criminal Justice Act, the Education Act and Ontario employment law, turned into short cards with the section cited.

Record and alert

One button records audio. Another sends your location to an emergency contact if a situation feels unsafe.

One tap to free legal help

Direct links to Legal Aid Ontario, Justice for Children and Youth and community legal clinics for follow-up.

Built for everyone

Big-text mode, read-aloud and English and French versions, so Elders and newcomers can use it as easily as teens.

Works offline

Every card is stored on the phone. No signal in a basement office or a parking lot doesn't stop it.

Outcomes

What changes

  • Short termUsers stay calm under pressure, avoid saying the wrong thing, and get answers without digging through long websites.
  • Long termPeople who understand their legal boundaries, fewer unlawful searches and workplace violations, and earlier connections between vulnerable people and the justice system.

Know your rights · Ontario

Tap a card to see the law behind the script.

These are the same cards that appear in the app. The front tells you what to say. The back explains why, and cites the law.

Stay safe: never physically resist police or staff, even if you believe something is unfair. Say your script calmly, remember the details, and challenge it later with a lawyer. This site and app give legal information, not legal advice.

Target users

Anyone who can't call a lawyer in the moment.

Most people run into police, school rules or a bad boss at some point. Pocket Rights focuses on the groups who meet them most often and have the fewest resources.

Students

High school students

Searches, suspensions and police in schools.

"The VP wants to search my locker."

Teens

Young people in public

Stops on the street, in parks and in cars, where youth have extra protections.

"Officers stopped us outside the plaza."

Young workers

First and part-time jobs

Breaks, pay stubs and unsafe tasks, where new workers often don't know the rules.

"No break on an eight-hour shift."

Elders

Seniors and newcomers

Big text, read-aloud and French support make the same cards usable for everyone.

"I can't read the small print."

Integration with the justice sector

We point people to the help that already exists.

Pocket Rights doesn't replace lawyers or clinics. It gets people through the first few minutes, then hands them to the organizations built to help. Partners would review our scripts for accuracy and receive referrals from the app.

Referral · Free lawyer

Legal Aid Ontario

Free duty counsel for anyone arrested or detained, and legal aid for people with low income. The app's "Talk to a lawyer" button connects here.

legalaid.on.ca

Justice for Children and Youth

A legal clinic mainly for people under 18, helping with school discipline, police and housing. Linked from every school and youth card.
